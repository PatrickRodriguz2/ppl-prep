Two exams, one app

The private pilot test (PAR) and the commercial pilot test (CAX) each have their own question bank, Library notes, mock test and progress. Choose the one you are studying for when you first open the app, and change with the switch at the top of Home or the Library. One Full Access purchase covers both, and each exam has its own free sample. The commercial bank adds compensation and common carriage, complex and high-performance endorsements, constant-speed propellers, retractable gear, turbocharging and pressurization, commercial maneuvers and high-altitude operations.

Exam-faithful mocks

PAR: 60 questions with the real 2-hour countdown. CAX: 100 questions with the real 2.5-hour countdown. Questions are weighted across the seven subjects (the FAA publishes no subject split for the commercial test, so its weights follow the spread of the ACS areas) — regulations, weather, airspace and airports, navigation, aircraft and systems, performance and weight and balance, and human factors — with generated nav-log, weight and balance and weather blocks, flagging and a question palette. No feedback until you submit, exactly like exam day, then the result against the real 70% standard of either test, with a per-subject breakdown for diagnosis and the ACS codes you missed ready to study.

A nav log generator, not one stale example

Every session plans a brand-new VFR cross-country between real United States airports — winds aloft, variation, true airspeed, fuel flow and a compass deviation card — and asks the questions computed from that log: magnetic heading, groundspeed, time en route, ETA, minimum fuel, compass heading and endurance. Infinite practice, never memorized answers.

Loading sheets, METARs and TAFs

A weight-and-balance trainer loads a four-seat trainer and asks for takeoff weight, center of gravity, envelope status, landing CG and the weight-shift fix — about a third of the sheets are deliberately out of limits. A weather trainer generates coherent METAR and TAF pairs and asks you to decode them and decide whether the flight is VFR, special VFR or neither.

Smart review that doesn't forget

Every answer is tracked. Missed questions return on a spaced schedule until they stick, and the dashboard estimates your readiness against the 70% pass mark of the exam you are studying — by subject and by topic, so you always know what to study next.
